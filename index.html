02 / White Space

The gap nobody else is filling

SBQuantum has no true one-to-one competitor. What exists is a well-funded field of quantum-navigation players, and the analysis below is the full read behind the conclusion, not just the conclusion, so the positioning stands up to scrutiny.

Competitive environment
The field is consolidating inside the US DIU Transition of Quantum Sensing program, an 18-performer cohort. Separately, the NV-diamond and vector-sensing ground is now contested by a direct peer with an ESA space program. SBQuantum does not win by out-spending that field. It wins on the one thing none of them can claim: a sensor operating in orbit today, and one you can actually buy.

03 / Targeting and Audience

Who feels this most, and how we reach them

Budget and effort follow warmth. Because this is a first paid program with no warm audience yet, the primary launch audience is the curated account list, targeted at the account level so engagement inside named companies is visible in reporting. Cold, broad targeting is held in reserve.

How we capture and prioritize: three pockets

Pocket 1 · Warm and known

CRM, trade-show contacts, and inbound relationships, uploaded as Matched Audiences. Smallest pool, easiest traction, starts first.

Pocket 2 · Curated account list (ABM)

The named target-account list, stratified by use case. The primary structural lever. Broad firmographic targeting will not reach this audience, so the list is required, not optional.

Pocket 3 · Retargeting and engagement

Video viewers, site visitors, and lead-form openers. Builds automatically once campaigns run, then feeds the warm tier continuously.

Sequenced across the funnel

Trust before CTA. The same account hears a different message depending on how warm it is.

Awareness
Lead with the in-orbit proof and the open-sensor frame (P1, P2). No ask. The goal is a view, a follow, a pool. Cold audiences meet the deployment and the idea that this is a buyable sensor.
Trust
Prove it technically (P2, P3). To everyone who engaged: the technical brief, map-resolution detail, publications, and the agency deployment. Credibility before any request.
Convert
One clear, low-friction ask (P1 + offer). Only to the warmest accounts, who have already met the proof. A capabilities conversation with the team running the deployment.

  • ICP versus non-ICP delivery, flagged for suppression

Channel and activation

  • LinkedIn performance by campaign, against its audience tier
  • Warm-pool growth over time
  • Cost per qualified action, and the first cost per MQL from the form

Activation triggers

Named account engages the deployment video → add to the 90-day retargeting pool
Engager opens the technical brief → move to the 30-day high-intent pool for the form
Warm account submits the lead gen form → log the MQL, hand to sales for first contact inside 24 hours
Honest limit
There is no closed-loop revenue attribution in the pilot, because there is no CRM integration in scope. Pipeline is tracked against the DemandSense account view and a manual log. We say so rather than imply a precision we do not have.
